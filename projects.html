Sankofa

Feature Film Screenplay, Historical Drama (119 Pages)

Logline: After surviving an attempted honor killing by her father, the mad Warlord Alaafin Ofinran, a rejected Oyo princess must rise from the brutal depths of the slave trade, unite with her estranged uncle, and lead a ragtag army to save the Kingdom of Benin from her father's gunpowder-fueled annihilation.

Creator's Statement: Sankofa is in the vein of The Woman King meets Gladiator, with the thematic depth of a Shakespearean tragedy like The Winter's Tale. The film aims to elevate sidelined narratives and restore balance to mainstream media. By exploring the rich, untold history of the Oyo Empire and the rise of its first female Alaafin, the film unites audiences through powerful cinematic storytelling and reclaims the cinematic truth of our past.

Sennuwy

Proof of Concept Short Film (30 Minutes), Pre-Production (Crewing Up)
Extracted from the full-length feature screenplay.

Logline: Two refugee sisters are taken in by their wealthy aunt and reverend uncle in suburban Atlanta, only to discover their sanctuary is a gilded cage of servitude and religious hypocrisy. When one sister catches the eye of a fashion photographer, she must weaponize her newfound confidence to shatter her unpayable debt, shed her stolen identity, and reclaim her autonomy.

Creator's Statement: At its core, this film examines the insidious machinery of weaponized gratitude and sanctified servitude: a reality quietly pervasive within the upwardly mobile diaspora, where human exploitation hides behind the polished veneer of Christian charity and middle-class respectability. Sponsoring vulnerable relatives from abroad is too often treated not as familial solidarity, but as a transaction: an assumed purchase that incurs an infinite moral debt. In this world, every warm meal, clean bed, and consular stamp becomes leverage to demand unconditional submission. Cruelty is spiritualized as godly discipline, and native names, heritage, and autonomy are systematically stripped away in the name of salvation.

What makes this domestic trap uniquely devastating is how it weaponizes the victims' own survival instincts against them. We watch two sisters fracture under the same roof: one coerced into policing the other just to preserve a fragile sense of safety, while the other refuses to let her spirit be colonized.

Cinematically, our visual language reflects this psychological war. We trap Abunaw inside the clinical, suffocating stillness of suburban Atlanta: immaculate baseboards, oppressive symmetry, and sterile lemon-waxed surfaces that function as an architectural cage. Against that quiet violence, her inner transformation into Sennuwy erupts as pure cinematic revolt. Through searing strobes, tactile camera work, and high-fashion visual transcendence, the film moves from captive silence to absolute sovereignty. This is not merely a story about surviving abuse; it is about tearing up an unpayable debt, shedding a stolen identity, and resurrecting an ancestral power that can never be owned.

Original IP In Development

The Sankofa Cosmos

Original Universe / Multi-Genre Media Ecosystem

The World: A soaring alternate history where Africa seized its own destiny through a unified, continent-wide war of liberation. In the 21st century, the Unified Doors of Africa (UDA) stands as a 1.5-billion-strong continental superpower guided by the ancient philosophy of Ma'at: a nation where borders were redrawn by historians, not conquerors, and where the extraordinary is grounded in complex political and social reality.

Creator's Statement: The Sankofa Cosmos is not a single narrative, but a new baseline reality. It moves beyond conventional inclusion to establish a paradigm of authentic centricity. By rooting political thrillers, high-concept sci-fi, and mythological fantasy in the unique history and soil of the UDA, we are not just adapting existing archetypes: we are forging original heroes and modern myths for a global audience.

House of Scorpions

Transmedia Political Thriller Project

Logline: Betrayed by the leader he created, a visionary architect in a fledgling African superpower launches a ruthless crusade to seize the nation and forge the global empire he was denied.

Format & Strategy: An epic narrative ecosystem built around a prestige television series and anchored by an innovative transmedia launch strategy. It kicks off with a standalone, feature-length live broadcast event, The FBC KXORM Secondaries Debate, broadcast via the Fabaraka Broadcasting Corporation with real-time in-universe social media and digital campaign ecosystems.

Tone: The Machiavellian chess game of House of Cards, played at the speed of sound in a world of Afro-futurist armor. Driving a concept Ferrari armored in Vibranium.

The Bahekaya Chronicles

Mythological Fantasy Franchise

The World: Inspired by the question of what a fully developed magical Africa could look like, The Bahekaya creates an entirely new magical civilization from scratch. Rooted in millennia of history, African philosophy, and myth, it follows the Bahekaya, the "people of magic," governed by the Magical Assembly of the Unified Doors of Africa (MAUDA) and educated at the legendary Wagadu school of wandless magic across five distinct Ka.

The Sahel Abimbola Saga (Film Epic): Haunted by his legendary father's murder at the hands of the dark sorcerer Bilisi Elegbara, a brilliant but arrogant young Wagadu student forges a dangerous friendship with a mysterious new student, unaware he is embracing the very evil that shattered his world.

The Oni Abimbola Trilogy (Prequel Epic): A young boy, betrayed by his community and left for dead, rises from his own grave to lead a magical revolution against a colonialist regime, overthrowing a corrupt headmaster and fulfilling an ancient prophecy to reclaim the soul of Africa's greatest school.

The Akofena Revolutionary Cosmos (ARC)

Superhero Franchise Ecosystem

The Foundation: ARC is a globally resonant superhero franchise rooted in the political, scientific, and mythological soil of the UDA. These heroes, known as The Revolutioners, struggle not just against external threats, but against the corruption of the original dream, holding the UDA accountable to its own sacred principles.

Captain Africa (Feature Film): The ghost of the first revolution. Sixty-seven years after the Unification War, Africa's legendary super-soldier is awakened to be a symbol of peace, only to discover he is a living weapon haunted by a past he cannot remember.

Firstlight (Feature Film): Africa's answer to Superman. A brilliant FACOS astronaut and astrophysicist imbued with the power of a nascent star returns to Earth as a radiant symbol of hope. His struggle poses a philosophical revolution: does one good man have the right to unilaterally change the world?

Shango (Feature Trilogy): An ancient god, imprisoned for a forbidden act of compassion, must bind himself to a mortal woman to stop a vengeful engineer from unearthing a world-breaking divine weapon.
